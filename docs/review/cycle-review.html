Review sheet: how the app thinks

Hi Drashti! This sheet shows every number and sentence the app uses for your cycle, with small pictures and examples worked out by the app itself. Nothing here is final until you say so.

What to do: in each box tick Looks right or Needs a change (write what it should be). Boxes marked Needs your yes are medical numbers or sentences that only you can confirm; each has links to where the number comes from. At the end press Copy my answers and send them to Tirth.

You do not need to know anything about computers. Just check it as a nurse. New since last time: the age question, what the app says when a period is late or nothing has been told for a long time, honest ranges, the typical (middle) cycle, and no ovulation guess for teenagers or very irregular cycles.

E. How wide the "next period" range is

Before, a woman with four identical 28-day cycles got a range of only 1 day each side. Real cycles vary by days even in regular women, so the range is now never narrower than 3 days each side, until she has 6 cycles (then 2 days).

H. Ovulation and fertile days

All guesses made from period dates only, always labelled as guesses. Not shown for under 20, or when cycles change by more than 7 days.

Needs your yes When ovulation happens

The app says / does: 14 days before the next period starts.

In real life the length of the second half of the cycle varies from person to person, which is why the guess can be days off.

Where this comes from: Human Reproduction 2024: luteal phase length · Bull 2019: 600,000 app-tracked cycles

Needs your yes Fertile days

The app says / does: From 5 days before ovulation to 1 day after it.

Sperm can live up to about 5 days. The egg lives about 12 to 24 hours.

Where this comes from: Wilcox 1995, NEJM: timing of sex and ovulation

Needs your yes "Lower-fertility days" (estimate)

The app says / does: Days outside the fertile days, with 2 days extra on each side. Only for regular cycles, always with "not birth control".

We never use the words "safe period" because these guesses can be wrong.
